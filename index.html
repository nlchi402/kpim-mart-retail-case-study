Part A. Project overview

Business scenario

KPIM Mart is used as a fictional retail business scenario. The project began with a sales-focused dataset and explored how a centralized business intelligence solution could connect commercial performance with inventory efficiency, supplier and logistics costs, receivables, payables, and working-capital management.

Business challenge: Extend the source data into an integrated retail analytical model and build a reporting system that supports yearly, monthly, and daily sales monitoring together with inventory, supplier-cost, and capital-efficiency analysis.

Sales PerformanceRevenue, deductions, COGS, gross profit, targets, and growth.
InventoryReceipts, issues, stock value, DIO, and turnover.
Supplier & Logistics CostFreight, purchase value, payables, and payment cycle.
Working CapitalAR, AP, Inventory, DSO, DPO, DIO, CCC, and Working Capital.

Excel prototyping and validation

Before building the Power BI model, I used Excel as a transparent prototyping and reconciliation layer. This allowed business rules to be tested at row level, master data to be mapped consistently, and source totals to be reviewed before the logic was transferred into Power Query and DAX.

How Excel was used

  • Structured Excel Tables kept formulas and references consistent across the Sales, Inventory, Supplier, Accounts Receivable, and Accounts Payable datasets.
  • XLOOKUP mapped product codes to product names, unit costs, and selling prices; linked stores to warehouses; and matched customer names with customer IDs.
  • SUMIFS aggregated daily inventory movements into weekly warehouse–product balances and summarised supplier, receivable, and payable values by business key and reporting period.
  • PivotTables were used during the mock-up and review stage to compare totals by product, warehouse, store, supplier, and period.

Outcome

  • Created consistent product, customer, store, supplier, and warehouse mappings across the 12 source tables.
  • Produced traceable row-level calculations for inventory receipts, issues, on-hand quantities, unit costs, and inventory values.
  • Established reconciliation checkpoints between daily and weekly inventory, sales and targets, and AR/AP balances before dashboard development.
  • Provided a validated baseline for checking Power Query transformations and Power BI/DAX outputs, reducing the risk of carrying mapping or aggregation errors into the final reports.

Synthetic data design

The synthetic extensions were designed around connected retail business rules rather than isolated random values. Their purpose is to demonstrate how a sales-focused source can be expanded into an operational and working-capital reporting model.

Connected business rules

  • Sales transactions generate Gross Sales, Sales Deduction, Net Sales, COGS, Gross Profit, and return values.
  • Warehouse receipts increase inventory and create supplier purchase and payable records.
  • Warehouse issues reduce inventory quantities and values and are reconciled to product movement.
  • Credit sales create receivables; collections reduce AR ending balances.
  • AR, AP, and ending inventory feed Working Capital and Cash Conversion Cycle calculations.
  • The monthly target source is reshaped from a wide month-by-column layout into a period-based structure for time analysis.

Analytical grain

  • Sales: one transaction line by order, product, customer, store, and warehouse.
  • Daily Inventory: one warehouse movement by document, posting date, and product.
  • Weekly Inventory: one warehouse–product balance by reporting week.
  • Accounts Payable: one supplier purchase/document balance with payment and aging attributes.
  • Accounts Receivable: one customer balance by reporting period with collection and aging attributes.
  • Sales Targets: one store–month planning value after transformation.

Part C. Power BI solution

Solution architecture

The solution uses Excel as the initial prototyping and reconciliation layer, followed by Power Query for data preparation, a relational analytical model for business relationships, and a documented layer of 70 DAX and analytical measures. This structure makes the calculation logic traceable from row-level Excel checks through to executive and operational Power BI reporting.

Design principle: Business rules are first tested and reconciled in Excel; Power Query standardises the source; KPIs are calculated at raw value before display-unit conversion; and time and comparison measures use consistent filter context so results remain reconcilable.

Interactive features

Data exploration

  • Filters by year, month, date, store, product, customer, supplier, and warehouse.
  • Bookmarks to switch Store, Employee, Customer, Brand, Supplier, and Storage views.
  • Drill-down through summarized KPI tables.
  • Navigation buttons across report pages.

Comparison and control

  • Last Year, Last Month, and Target comparisons.
  • As of Date logic for Daily Turnover.
  • Rolling 12 months for Inventory Turnover, DIO, Payable Turnover, and DPO.
  • Display-unit selection and KPI conditional formatting.

1. Yearly Sales Report

The report provides an annual overview combining actual results, prior-year comparison, and target achievement.

Main content

  • Gross Sales, Net Sales, Target, Sales Deduction, COGS, and Gross Profit.
  • Customers generating sales and returns.
  • Monthly trend and Last Year comparison.
  • Detailed KPI table by selected dimension.

Features and value

  • Switch Store, Employee, Customer, and Brand views.
  • YoY comparison and % Gross Profit monitoring.
  • Identify major contributors and declining groups.
  • Support annual planning and resource allocation.

2. Monthly Sales Report

The report focuses on the selected month, allowing users to monitor short-term results and explain changes versus the previous month.

Main content

  • Net Sales, Gross Profit, Sales Deduction, and Target Achievement.
  • Weekly movement within the month.
  • Performance by store, product, customer, and brand.
  • Detailed KPIs versus Last Month.

Features and value

  • MoM comparison within the same filter context.
  • Identify unusual weeks or business groups.
  • Review the effect of pricing and promotion activity.
  • Support in-month corrective action.

3. Daily Turnover Report

Daily Turnover monitors sales progress up to a selected day, enabling managers to respond quickly to short-term changes.

Main content

  • Net Sales YTD/MTD and % Gross Profit YTD/MTD.
  • Net Sales Achievement, Daily AVG MTD Sales, and Daily AVG MTG Sales.
  • Working Days and Working Days Left.
  • Daily revenue and gross-profit trend.

Features and value

  • As of Date controls cumulative results and monthly target context.
  • Filters by Employee, Store, and Category.
  • Identify abnormal daily revenue or margin movements.
  • Estimate the required run rate for remaining working days.

4. Inventory Management

The report connects stock quantity, value, and movement speed to evaluate warehouse operating efficiency.

Main content

  • On-hand Quantity, On-hand Value, and Average Inventory Value.
  • In Value, Out Value, DIO, and Inventory Turnover.
  • Closing inventory comparison with Last Year.
  • Product table with Beginning Value, EOP, Unit Cost, and turnover.

Features and value

  • Switch Supplier and Storage views.
  • Switch Inventory Quantity and Inventory Value perspectives.
  • Identify high stock, slow movement, and imbalances.
  • Support replenishment and stock-transfer planning.

5. Inventory Cost

The report extends inventory management into logistics cost and supplier payment obligations.

Main content

  • Freight In, Freight Out, FI/Purchase, and FO/Sales.
  • Payable Turnover 12 months and DPO.
  • On-hand Value compared with Last Year.
  • Supplier and warehouse cost table.

Features and value

  • Compare by Storage or Supplier.
  • Analyze monthly cost and DPO trends.
  • Conditional formatting highlights unusual payment periods.
  • Support supplier review and logistics-cost control.

6. Working Capital Report

The report connects receivables, payables, and inventory to provide an integrated view of working-capital efficiency and the cash-conversion cycle.

Main content

  • DSO, DPO, DIO, and CCC.
  • AR Ending Balance, AP Ending Balance, Inventory, and Working Capital.
  • In-year trends and Last Year comparisons.
  • Aging, supplier, category, and capital-movement analysis.

Features and value

  • Monitor all working-capital components on one page.
  • Identify capital tied up in receivables or inventory.
  • Assess the use of supplier payment terms.
  • Support cash-flow and credit-policy decisions.

Part E. Insights and management value

Illustrative insights and recommended actions

The patterns below demonstrate how the dashboard can be interpreted. They are illustrative decision scenarios derived from synthetic data and do not represent actual company performance.

Illustrative patternBusiness interpretationRecommended action
MTD Net Sales are below the required monthly run rate.The current daily sales pace may be insufficient to achieve the monthly target.Prioritize stores or categories with the largest target gaps and monitor the required average sales for the remaining working days.
DIO contributes a large share of CCC.Working capital may be concentrated in slow-moving or excess inventory.Review slow-moving SKUs, replenishment levels, and stock-transfer opportunities between warehouses or stores.
Revenue contribution is high but Gross Profit contribution is comparatively low.A high-sales category may be diluting overall margin through product mix, pricing, or discounting.Review price, promotion, discount, and product-mix decisions together rather than optimizing revenue alone.

Receivable or payable balances move into older aging buckets.Collection risk or near-term payment obligations may be increasing.Prioritize collection follow-up, review customer credit terms, and sequence supplier payments using due-date and aging information.
